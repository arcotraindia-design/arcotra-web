For your home

Start with one room, or design the whole house.

Most people begin with the room that bothers them most. Others come to us before the house is even built. Both are a good place to start.

Kitchen

The room people ask about first. Layout, storage, worktops, lighting — planned around how you actually cook.

Living room

Seating that works for the room you have, not the one in the catalogue. Light, flow, and where the television really goes.

Bedroom

Wardrobes that fit, light that suits the hour, and enough space left to move around the bed.

Teen’s room

A room that has to be a bedroom, a desk and somewhere to have friends over — and that its owner will want to change again in two years. We plan for that.

Dream house

The whole home designed at once, so the kitchen, the hallway and the bedrooms belong to the same house instead of five separate decisions.

Bungalow house

Single-storey living has its own logic: every room touches the garden, the roof is free to open up, and nothing is hidden behind a staircase.

For institutions and businesses

Buildings full of rooms are a different problem.

A hostel is not a big house, and an academy is not a large office. Each one is solved differently — and knowing that difference is most of the job.

Student hostels

A hundred rooms, not one. The work is repetition done well — the same standard in every room, storage for each bed, and common areas that hold the building together. Finishes are chosen for five years of heavy use, not for the photograph.

Academies & training centres

Classrooms, labs and staff rooms, plus the corridors that have to absorb everyone at once when a class ends. Acoustics, sightlines and daylight decide whether a room can actually be taught in.

Offices & retail

Workspaces and shops for the people who spend all day in them. Layout follows how the team really works, not the floor plan the landlord handed over.

How it works

You will see the room before you pay for a single tile.

We often work with people we have never met in person, and it works well — because everything is agreed on screen before anyone spends money.

01

Tell us about the space

Send photos, a floor plan, or even a rough sketch with measurements. A WhatsApp voice note works too.

02

We design it in 3D

We plan the layout, choose materials and lighting, and build the room in three dimensions.

03

You see it before it exists

Walk through realistic views of your own room. Change the worktop, move the island, try another colour — while it still costs nothing.

04

You get the drawings

Final visuals and dimensioned drawings your builder or carpenter can work from, wherever you are.

The same kitchen, two materials. Deciding here costs nothing; deciding after the cabinets are made costs a great deal.

Where it starts

Every project begins as a plan.

Before anything is rendered, the layout is worked out on paper: where the walls go, which way the doors swing, how much room is left to walk around the table.

Get this part right and the rest follows. Get it wrong and no amount of beautiful rendering will save the room.

About

A small studio that takes on a few projects at a time.

Arcotra is a design studio in Noida, India. We design interiors in 3D — kitchens, living rooms, bedrooms and complete homes — and hand over drawings that a builder can work from, wherever the project is.

Alongside homes we design student hostels and academies — work where the challenge is repetition and wear rather than one perfect room.

We are deliberately small. That means you talk to the person doing the design, and it means we take on a few projects at a time rather than many at once.

Nearly all of our clients come through someone they trust. We would rather keep it that way than chase volume.
